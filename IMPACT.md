# Impact

ScamShield LK is an adviser-support prototype. A career adviser pastes a recruitment chat a student has already received, and a person reviews uncertain chats, unfamiliar chats, and explicit requests for a password or one-time code. On the inspected new-tactic batch the classifier itself is right on 30 of 40 chats. The final wording line is right on 40 of 40, and 20 of those 40 still go to review. On a one-time fresh set the classifier is right on 14 of 16 and the final line on 16 of 16, with 7 of 16 reviewed. Ordinary onboarding chats are labelled correctly and still all go to review. The figures below are published counts of who is exposed. They are not product results, and we are not claiming a rupee total prevented.

## Who

The people in front of this tool are students and school leavers looking for a first job, and other job seekers who get a part-time or work-from-home offer in a chat app.

The Ministry of Higher Education's 2024 progress report says about **444,057** students were enrolled in the state higher education system, of whom **143,203** were in state universities on Z-score selection. For the 2023 G.C.E. Advanced Level, **173,444** students were eligible for university and the system expected to enrol **43,204**. In 2024, Mahapola and bursary support reached **92,550** students. A deposit of a few thousand rupees is a serious loss for that household.

## Harm already on record

On 28 June 2025 the Daily Mirror reported a CID warning about fake work-from-home schemes and fake remote jobs used to collect bank accounts. It cites Sri Lanka CERT: **7,210** online-scam complaints in 2024, about **1,440** of them financial fraud, including job-offer fraud. In the first quarter of 2025 CERT received **2,887** cybercrime complaints, **364** of them financial fraud. In February 2025, CERT warned that fake job ads were collecting identity documents.

The Central Bank's consumer booklet uses this same pattern as a teaching story: a job offer, then a cash deposit. In that story the family deposits **Rs. 150,000** and never hears back. That is an illustration, not a national loss total.

On 2 June 2026 the Central Bank launched **Be Scam Proof** month. The Governor named students and young people among the groups the campaign has to reach, and described scams as wiping out household savings. Assistant Governor W.A. Dilrukshini included digital fraud and account-lending in the same list, and said that when one tactic becomes known, another appears. That is why this project keeps a shifted set.

## A pilot that fits the product

The first deployment is one university career centre, not a national bot and not the 143,203 undergraduates. Staff paste a chat the student brings to the desk. The tool shows a scam signal or the absence of one, the phrases it used, and the score after each message. Unfamiliar wording, an uncertain score, or a request for a password or one-time code goes to a person at the desk. We do not open links or contact the recruiter. The wording score is a draft for that person. It is not a decision that a job is safe.

What a pilot would count, and what we do not have yet: chats checked per week, cases where the student had been asked to pay and the tool showed a scam signal, and deposits the student then chose not to send.

A later contact line, run by CERT or a career-guidance unit, can use the same check on messages people forward. That line is not built. Synthetic chats stay in this repo. They are not posted and not sent to real numbers. The Central Bank's Be Scam Proof coalition is the kind of partner that already talks to this audience. No pilot agreement exists today.
